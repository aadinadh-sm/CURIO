✳ PATTERN DISCOVERY

What tends to happen
before a problem?

CURIO compares the early and late parts of independent checks to find signals that recur before pressure is detected.

↗
How discovery works

CURIO compares changes across separate sessions, then looks for trends that appear more often in pressure sessions than in no-pressure sessions. Before human-verified labels are available, those groups come from CURIO’s own screening rules, so the results are exploratory and can inherit the rules’ blind spots.

ASSOCIATION IS NOT CAUSATION · EXPLORATORY UNTIL TESTED ON HELD-OUT SESSIONS
✳ A PLAIN-LANGUAGE GUIDE

How CURIO works.

Short, local, and honest about what it can—and cannot—conclude.

01Observe

Samples CPU, memory, disk I/O, network rates, process context, and available temperatures once a second for 30 seconds.

02Summarize

Groups readings into six five-second windows. It compares averages, peaks, changes, available RAM, throughput, disk busy time and response time.

03Explain

Shows evidence for observable operating conditions. Without a trained model, these are transparent screening rules—not ML diagnoses or calibrated probabilities.

04Discover

After enough separate checks, compares early-to-late trends between pressure and no-pressure sessions. A repeated association is a lead to investigate, not proof of cause.

What this first version cannot conclude

It cannot confirm a failing hardware component, diagnose network latency or packet loss, prove thermal throttling, or reliably catch a slowdown that does not happen during the 30-second check. Temperature and some disk counters may not be available from every Windows device.
